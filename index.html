📱 Slide 1 • What the Phone is Made Of

The amount of metals in a device and what the main ones are used for.

Overview

Metals in Your Phone

Around a quarter to a third of your phone's weight is pure metal:

40 to 50 grams of metals Accounts for 25% to 30% of the smartphone's total weight.
Over 60 chemical elements A large part of the periodic table packed into the palm of your hand.
What They Do

The Function of Each Metal

The practical role of each metal in everyday phone operation:

Copper (~16 g) • Electricity Carries power and electric signals throughout the entire device.
Gold (14 to 34 mg) • Perfect Contact Never rusts or oxidizes, ensuring chips and processors never lose signal.
Palladium (~15 mg) • Heat Resistance Helps manage extreme temperatures and voltage stability.

☣️ Slide 3 • The Three Dangers

Chemical threats that happen when e-waste is discarded in common trash or open dumps.

🧪 Biological Contamination

Dangerous Materials

Lead in older solders, mercury in displays, and cadmium in microchips and batteries.

Bioaccumulates in the body: enters the food chain and causes serious health damage.
💧 Water Contamination

Water Pollution

Rainwater washes over exposed scrap in dumps, dissolving toxic chemicals into the soil.

Dark toxic liquid seeps into the ground, reaching groundwater and drinking wells.
🔥 Atmospheric Pollution

Burning Plastic Releases Dioxins

Open-air burning to extract copper from wires releases smoke packed with organochlorines.

Linked to cancer: toxic smoke travels with the wind and harms nearby communities.

♻️ Slide 6 • The Concept of WEEE & REEE

Any unused device with a plug, cord, or battery — and the official 4-line classification.

Everyday Rule

Technological Waste

Any device with a plug, cord, battery, or circuit that we no longer use.

From earbuds to refrigerators, they blend synthetic polymers, toxins, and precious metals.

Rule of thumb: Has a plug, cord, battery, or circuit? It's e-waste.
Official Definition

End of Functional Life

Electronic and electrical equipment that is obsolete, broken, or has lost functional use.

WEEE Waste Electrical & Electronic Equipment International Standard
REEE Resíduos Eletroeletrônicos Brazilian Legislation (PNRS)
Classification in 4 Lines
Green Line

IT & Mobile: Computers, laptops, tablets, and smartphones.

Brown Line

Audio & Video: TVs, monitors, soundbars, and audio gear.

White Line

Home Appliances: Refrigerators, stoves, and washing machines.

Blue Line

Portables & Tools: Small electric appliances and power tools.

🔋 Slide 7 • Hope: Science is Taking Action

Breakthrough research showing where recycling technology is heading.

🚗 Clean Mobility

Electric Vehicle Batteries

In laboratories, recycling recovers aluminum and lithium. Roughly estimated, it spares about 1 ton of rock per battery.

Recovers aluminum and lithium. Nickel and cobalt are preserved for reuse.
💻 Microelectronics

Processor Recycling

New recovery and refining methods allow reclaiming and recycling chips with 70% to 99% efficiency.

Reclaims 70% to 99% of precious metals and vital components.
🥇 Olympic Proof

Tokyo: 5,000 Medals

78,000 tons of donated electronics were recycled into 5,000 Olympic medals.

Roughly 45,000 tons of virgin rock were spared from extraction (estimate).

These technologies need raw material. The metal must reach them — and right now, it is sitting in someone's drawer.

✅ Slide 8 • What to Do: The Practical Guide

Everyday actions divided into 2 phases to close the mineral loop.

Phase 1

In Use & Maintenance

🛠️ Care before replacing Update the software and replace the worn battery before considering a new device.
🛒 Circular consumption Buy refurbished. Return old car batteries to the retailer when buying a new one.
Phase 2

In Conscious Disposal

🗄️ Don't leave it idle Hand over what you don't use. Idle devices in drawers can also pose risks.
📍 Take to a collection point Bring to authorized drop-off points (PEVs). Check hours and accepted items in advance.
🌍 UN Estimate: If the World Recycled Everything 900 million tons of virgin rock spared

If all e-waste generated in 2022 were recycled, around 900 million tons of rock would never need to be mined.
